# Belt Conveyor and Transfer Line – Technician Manual

Applies to the flat belt conveyor with motor-driven head pulley, part carriers and the transfer point to the next station.
All work described here is for trained maintenance technicians only.

## Belt Slip

**Symptoms**
- Drive motor runs but the belt moves slower than set or stops under load.
- Squealing noise at the drive pulley; black rubber dust under the head pulley.
- Parts arrive late at the next station, cycle time increases.

**Related error codes:** E201 (Conveyor belt slip)

**Step-by-step fix**
1. Stop the conveyor and apply lockout/tagout.
2. Remove the guard at the head pulley.
3. Inspect the drive pulley lagging. Replace the pulley if the lagging is worn smooth, cracked or peeling.
4. Clean oil, grease or product residue from the belt underside and the pulley surface.
5. Check belt tension: the belt should deflect no more than 1–2 % of the span between rollers when pressed at mid span.
6. Adjust the take-up screws evenly on both sides so the belt tracks centered.
7. Refit the guard, remove lockout/tagout, and run the conveyor empty for five minutes while watching tracking.
8. Run it under full load and confirm the speed in the line controller.

**Safety note:** Apply lockout/tagout before any work: switch off the drive, lock the main disconnect and attach your personal tag. Never reach into a moving belt or pulley; pinch points at the pulleys can pull in hands and clothing.

## Part Jam at Transfer Point

**Symptoms**
- Parts pile up or get stuck where the conveyor hands over to the next station.
- Transfer light barrier stays blocked; upstream conveyor stops on back-pressure.
- Part carriers are tilted or scratched at the same position.

**Related error codes:** E202 (Part jam at transfer point)

**Step-by-step fix**
1. Stop the conveyor and the receiving station, and apply lockout/tagout to both.
2. Remove the stuck parts by hand. Check them for deformation and put damaged parts in the reject bin.
3. Inspect the guide rails at the transfer point. Measure the gap to the part with a gauge; it must be 2–3 mm on each side.
4. Realign the guide rails and tighten the clamp bolts. Replace bent rails.
5. Inspect the part carriers for deformed or broken pockets. Remove damaged carriers from the line.
6. Check the height difference between the conveyor and the receiving surface. It must not exceed 1 mm.
7. Clean the transfer light barrier lenses and check its alignment.
8. Remove lockout/tagout, run single parts through the transfer at slow speed, then return to production speed.

**Safety note:** Apply lockout/tagout before reaching into the transfer point. Both the conveyor and the receiving station must be locked out, because either one can push parts into the gap.

## Drive Motor Overtemperature

**Symptoms**
- Motor protection trips; the conveyor stops and restarts only after cooling down.
- Motor housing is too hot to touch; burning smell.

**Related error codes:** E203 (Drive motor overtemperature)

**Step-by-step fix**
1. Stop the conveyor and apply lockout/tagout. Let the motor cool down.
2. Clean the motor fan cover and the cooling fins. Remove dust, cardboard or plastic film that blocks airflow.
3. Turn the motor shaft by hand (with the belt released) and listen for bearing noise.
4. Check for sustained overload: belt tension too high, seized rollers, or more parts on the belt than designed.
5. Turn every return roller by hand and replace rollers that do not turn freely.
6. Check the motor terminal box for loose or discolored connections (electrician only).
7. Remove lockout/tagout, start the conveyor and measure the motor current. Compare it with the rated current on the motor nameplate.

**Safety note:** Apply lockout/tagout before any work. Only a qualified electrician may open the terminal box or measure on live circuits.
